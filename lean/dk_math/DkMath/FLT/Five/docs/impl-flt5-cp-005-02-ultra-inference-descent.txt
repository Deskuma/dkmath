*active*

This order is Level II.  It begins only after the exact factor packet from cp-005-01
compiles.  The task is not merely to simplify equations.  The task is to discover a
strictly smaller packet or a contradiction while preserving every certified invariant.

3. Mandatory finite obstruction in the odd branch
======================================================================

For the odd branch:

```text
f^5 = e^5 + 4*d^5
```

prove the exact mod-11 theorem:

```lean
theorem eleven_dvd_d_of_fifth_add_four_fifth
    {e d f : ℕ}
    (h : e ^ 5 + 4 * d ^ 5 = f ^ 5) :
    11 ∣ d
```

A finite `Fin 11` classification with `decide +kernel` is acceptable.  A short theorem
using fifth-power residues modulo 11 is preferable if Mathlib already exposes the
needed API.

Then combine it with the packet fields:

```text
11 does not divide c
11 does not divide e*f
11 does not divide e
11 does not divide f.
```

The reason is `Coprime c d` and the exact product `e*f=5^5*c^8`.

Do not call `11 ∣ d` a contradiction.  It is a forced primitive channel and should be
fed into the next valuation/factor analysis.

The latter two are not direct ordinary differences of two fifth powers.  Explore
whether the factor packet supplies an alternative scaling, a sum orientation, or a

For the odd branch, prove the standard gcd control:

```text
gcd(f-e, GN5(f-e,e)) divides 5.
```

Reuse existing DkMath `GcdDiffPow`/GN lemmas if they match the concrete variables
without importing a circular FLT conclusion.

Since `11 != 5`, the forced 11-adic mass is owned by exactly one of:

```text
f-e

```

Derive the strongest exact valuation statement possible.  Candidate subcases include:

```text

  -> 11^(5*v11(d)) divides f-e
```

Search existing `padicValNat`, LTE, and primitive-beam APIs.  Implement only statements
whose hypotheses are actually established by the factor packet.

The objective of Route A is one of:


======================================================================
5. Route B — normalize the discriminant square inside GoldenInt
======================================================================

The quartic inversion gives:

```text
U^2 - 20*s^4 = (4*d^5)^2.
```

Use the integral basis `1,phi`, where `sqrt(5)=2*phi-1`.

The unnormalized element is:

```text
alpha = (U-2*s^2) + (4*s^2)*phi.
```

Its golden norm is:

```text
goldenNorm alpha = (4*d^5)^2.
```

Both coordinates are divisible by four.  Prefer the normalized element:

```text
M0 = (X^2 + 3*s^2)/4
N0 = s^2
alpha0 = M0 + N0*phi.
```

First prove integrality:

```text
4 divides X^2+3*s^2.
```

This follows from the primitive parity split:

- if `s` is odd, both squares are `1 mod 4`;
- if `s` is even, `X` and `s` are even.

Then prove exactly:

```text
goldenNorm alpha0 = d^10 = (d^5)^2.
```

Suggested transparent coordinate definition:

```lean
def zeroSectorSquareGolden (X s : ℤ) (hdiv : 4 ∣ X^2+3*s^2) : GoldenInt :=
  ⟨(X^2+3*s^2)/4, s^2⟩
```

Choose a formulation that avoids carrying proof arguments inside data if that makes
rewriting painful.

6. Control alpha0 and its conjugate
======================================================================

Prove the strongest relative-primality theorem available for `alpha0` and its golden
conjugate.

A common nonunit factor divides:

```text
alpha0
conj(alpha0)
alpha0-conj(alpha0), whose non-rational part is controlled by s^2
alpha0*conj(alpha0) = d^10.
```

Because `Coprime c d`, `s` and `d` share no prime.  The normalization should also remove
the visible factor four.  Investigate whether the normalized pair is genuinely
relatively prime or relatively prime up to a small explicitly classified factor.

Required discipline:

- Do not assert relative primality from the rational gcd alone.
- Reuse the explicit `GoldenRelPrime`, divisibility, conjugation, and Euclidean-domain
  infrastructure already developed in cp-004f/cp-004i.
- If an exceptional factor remains, identify it explicitly by norm and strip it before
  applying perfect-power splitting.
- Prove that any exceptional factor does not destroy strict descent.

The preferred result is:

```text
alpha0 and conj(alpha0) are relatively prime in GoldenInt.
```

An acceptable weaker result is a packet that removes a certified finite unit/ramifier
factor and leaves relatively prime factors.


Then substitute into the first coordinate and norm equations.  Search for:

11. Descend the phi square sector
======================================================================

For:

```text
s^2 = (m+n)^2+n^2,
```

this is a primitive or near-primitive Pythagorean equation after gcd normalization.

Compute:

```text
gcd(|m+n|,|n|) = gcd(|m|,|n|)
```

and establish the exact primitive condition supplied by the square factorization.
Classify parity, then use an existing Mathlib primitive Pythagorean parameterization if
it is practical.  Otherwise prove the concrete parameterization needed here:

```text
|m+n| = k*(p^2-q^2)
|n|   = k*(2*p*q)
|s|   = k*(p^2+q^2)
```

with exact restrictions on `k`, parity, and coprimality.

Feed these formulas into:

```text
goldenNorm gamma
alpha0 = plus or minus phi*gamma^2
```

and search for a smaller zero-sector packet or a contradiction with the already fixed
shape `|s|=5^6*c^10`.

The unusually large power structure of `s` is not optional data: every prime exponent
outside the fixed `5^6` layer is a multiple of ten.  Exploit that in the Pythagorean
factors.

12. Route C — systematic finite residue reconnaissance
======================================================================

Finite computation is authorized as a conjecture generator and false-claim detector.
Do not stop at a Python table or a `#eval` observation.

Search the complete factor packet, not only the branch equation, modulo carefully
chosen prime powers.  Useful coordinates include:

```text
r, s
X=2*r+s
c,d,e,f
N(r,s)=plus or minus b
A,B
```

Useful moduli include:

```text
11, 31, 41, 61, 101
25, 125, 625
8, 16, 32, 64
products of a small 2-power, 5-power, and one prime congruent to 1 mod 5
```

A modulus is valuable only if its obstruction can be stated as a small kernel-checked
classification theorem.

Required workflow:

1. enumerate residues externally or in a scratch Lean file;
2. include every packet equation and coprimality/parity condition representable modulo
   the chosen modulus;
3. identify a precise surviving or impossible class;
4. restate it as a theorem over `Fin n` or `ZMod n`;
5. prove it with `decide +kernel`, `norm_num`, or algebra;
6. lift it back to the integer packet.

Do not add giant opaque decision theorems without a mathematical explanation in source
comments.  Prefer several small classifications that visibly narrow the packet.

- retain any true reusable identities;

- continue with another route.

Examples of unacceptable stops:

```text

remaining theorem is that none of those four structures satisfies a displayed pair of
polynomial equations.
```

```text
remaining theorem is a displayed strict inequality needed to construct the smaller
packet.
```
