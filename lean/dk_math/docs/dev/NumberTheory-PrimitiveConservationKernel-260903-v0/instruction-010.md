## 6. Mathematical interpretation

The theorem formalizes the finite conservation law

$$
q\le P,
\qquad
0<m\le\operatorname{squareBody}(q)
$$

implying the dichotomy

$$
\boxed{
m\text{ is supported entirely by }\mathcal P_{\le P}
}
$$

or

$$
\boxed{
m=p\,k
}
$$

with

$$
p>P,
\qquad
p\text{ prime and fresh},
$$

$$
0<k\le P,
$$

$$
k\text{ supported entirely by }\mathcal P_{\le P},
$$

$$
\gcd(p,k)=1,
$$

and every fresh prime direction of `m` is equal to `p`.

Thus within the square conservation window:

> the old world can absorb arbitrary old-prime multiplicity, but it can carry
> at most one genuinely new prime direction, and that fresh direction occurs
> only once.

This is the intended PCK semantic theorem.

## 7. Important multiplicity wording

Do not say that the whole point `m` is squarefree.

The theorem allows arbitrary exponents among the old prime factors in `k`.

What is controlled is only the fresh direction:
- there is at most one fresh prime `p > P`;
- `p` does not divide the cofactor;
- therefore the fresh direction occurs to depth one.

This firewall is important because GN examples elsewhere in DkMath show that
generic primitive phenomena are not universally squarefree.

## 8. Relation to PCK-004

PCK-004 handles the extreme support-disjoint case. If

```lean

```

also holds in the certified fine square world, PCK-003/PCK-004 force `m`
itself to be prime and self-fresh.

PCK-008 is more general:
- it does not assume support disjointness;
- old support may remain in the bounded cofactor `k`;
- it classifies every positive point in the square window.

Do not add another support-disjoint corollary unless it is genuinely needed.
PCK-004 already owns that surface.
