## Implemented theorem

The exact public theorem is:

    theorem primitiveConservationKernel_dichotomy_of_le_fine_squareBody
        {q P m : ℕ}
        (hqP : q ≤ P)
        (hm : 0 < m)
        (hmUpper : m ≤ squareBody q) :
        PrimeScaleGeneratedBy (primeScalesUpTo P) m ∨
          ∃ p k,
            Nat.Prime p ∧
            P < p ∧
            0 < k ∧
            k ≤ P ∧
            FreshPrimeDirection (primeScalesUpTo P) m p ∧
            p * k = m ∧
            PrimeScaleGeneratedBy (primeScalesUpTo P) k ∧
            Nat.Coprime p k ∧
            (∀ ⦃r : ℕ⦄,
              FreshPrimeDirection (primeScalesUpTo P) m r → r = p)

The proof is exactly the intended two-step composition:

    have hmUpperCoarse : m ≤ squareBody P :=
      hmUpper.trans (squareBody_mono hqP)
    exact
      primeScaleGeneratedBy_or_uniqueFresh_small_split_of_le_squareBody
        hm hmUpperCoarse

The first step nests the fine square window in the coarse one. The second
step reuses the already-proved coarse dichotomy; no factorization, cofactor,
freshness, coprimality, or uniqueness theorem is reproved here.

## Semantic boundary

The first alternative says that `m` is entirely generated by the canonical
old world `primeScalesUpTo P`. The second says that `m = p * k` for one fresh
prime direction `p > P`, with `k` positive, bounded by `P`, old-generated,
and coprime to `p`; the final clause makes that fresh direction unique.

The freshness-depth statement is deliberately narrow. Since `p` is prime and
coprime to `k`, the fresh direction occurs to depth one in this split. This
does not say that `m` or `k` is squarefree, and it does not say that the fresh
branch occurs for every `m` in the finite window.

“Primitive” here means a fresh prime direction relative to the finite old
world. It is not a Zsigmondy primitive divisor, a `PrimitiveBeam` conclusion,
or an RH/analytic primitive. The module adds no generic `PrimitiveKernel`
definition or structure.

PCK-004 established a self-fresh endpoint under a support-disjointness
hypothesis. PCK-008 is the broader bounded dichotomy: it does not assume
support disjointness, and it preserves the old-generated branch when old
prime support is present.
