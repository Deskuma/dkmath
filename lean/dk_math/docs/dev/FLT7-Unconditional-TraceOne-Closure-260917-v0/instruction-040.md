prime `q` dividing both square-root norms, prove:

```text

ncard {P ∈ primesOver(q) | quotientSquareIdeal t ≤ P} = 2
```

The decisive input must be the exact R27 additive equation together with the
cyclic Galois action and the R33 xor allocation theorem.

This is not yet a valuation theorem, successor theorem, descent theorem, or
FLT7 contradiction.

## Part D — cyclic orbit equals the three primes above q

Use the checked Galois structure and Mathlib's Galois action on
`Ideal.primesOver`.  Prefer the existing invariant/pretransitive APIs such as


an explicit three-prime description, conceptually

```text

P2 := sigma^2 • P0
```

with:

- each `Pi` lying above `q`;
- `P0,P1,P2` pairwise distinct;
- every prime above `q` equal to one of `P0,P1,P2`.

Use the already checked facts

rather than quotient-evaluation calculations.

is acceptable.

## Part E — two gap primes force a third gap prime

Assume `P ≠ Q` are two primes above the same common norm prime `q`, and both
contain the gap-square-root principal ideal.

Using Parts C/D, transport the situation to one prime ideal `P*` that
contains two of the three concrete rotated roots

```text
r0
r1
r2.
```

Map the R27 equation into the actual ring of integers if needed:

```text
c0 * (r0^7)^2 + c1 * (r1^7)^2 + c2 * (r2^7)^2 = 0.
```

At `P*`:

1. the two terms corresponding to the two contained roots lie in `P*`;
2. hence the third weighted term lies in `P*`;
3. the third coefficient is a unit, so it does not lie in the proper prime
   ideal;
4. primality/radicality therefore forces the third rotated root into `P*`.

Expose a reusable theorem, conceptually:

```text
two_rotated_gap_roots_mem_imp_all_three_mem
```

or a theorem directly at the prime-allocation level.

Do not reduce coefficients modulo a rational prime by hand.  Their unit status
is the only coefficient property required here.

## Part F — at most one prime above q can contain the gap root

From Part E and the membership transport, show:

```text
if two distinct primes above q contain gapSquareIdeal t,
then every prime above q contains gapSquareIdeal t.
```

But R29 supplies a prime `Q` above `q` containing the quotient-square-root
ideal.

Apply the R33 theorem

```text
directOrbitSquareRefinement_prime_ideal_allocation_xor
```

at `Q`.

Since every prime above `q` would contain the gap ideal, `Q` would contain
both sides, contradicting xor.

Conclude:

```text
at most one prime above q contains gapSquareIdeal t.
```

R29 already gives at least one.  Therefore exactly one does.

## Part G — quotient side has exactly two primes

Define finite subsets of the three primes above `q`, conceptually:

```text
GapPrimes(q,t) :=
  {P ∈ primesOver(q) | gapSquareIdeal t ≤ P}

QuotientPrimes(q,t) :=
  {P ∈ primesOver(q) | quotientSquareIdeal t ≤ P}.
```

Use the R33 xor theorem to prove they partition `primesOver(q)`.

Then combine

```text
primesOver(q).ncard = 3
GapPrimes(q,t).ncard = 1
```

to prove

```text
QuotientPrimes(q,t).ncard = 2.
```

Do not re-prove the quotient cardinality from the twisted equation.

Expose stable public theorems for both cardinalities.
